# Goal B · United States

One of three Goal B markets (with India and Malaysia–Singapore), covering B2–B5. The US is the **value market**: the largest data-centre build-out in the
world, Schneider's biggest region, and customers whose own climate targets already count SF6.

## Executive summary

| Part | Agreed sentence |
|---|---|
| **Why the US** | The US has the largest data-centre build-out (≈ 50 GW operating, a pipeline ≈ 3× that) and is Schneider's largest region (39 % of revenue, +15 % organic); we lead in **Texas**, the fastest-growing state with no SF6 rule, through customers whose sites in California, New York or the EU already have to be SF6-free. |
| **B2 · Competition** | No major supplier has a confirmed SF6-free medium-voltage range certified to US standards yet, Eaton included, so the US is a **race to certify**: Schneider wins if it gets a US-certified AirSeT to data centres first, built in its US plants, inside the reference designs it already sells. |
| **B3 · Value proposition** | At the same price as SM6, AirSeT costs a data centre the same or less to own, removes SF6 from the Scope 1 that its net-zero target already counts, and lets it use one SF6-free design on every site for 40 years. |
| **B4 · Pricing and commercial** | Price at parity with SM6 while winning the first references, under the quote rules with an indexation clause, then earn a premium only through an optional monitoring and service package; build in the US to avoid tariffs and make AirSeT the default in Schneider's data-centre reference designs. |
| **B5 · Roadmap and win conditions** | Over 2027–2031, Schneider first certifies and pilots AirSeT with 2–3 data-centre customers in Texas (year 1), then follows them to their new sites and builds AirSeT into its reference designs (years 2–3), then covers 34.5 kV campuses with RM AirSeT 38 and enters Northern Virginia (years 4–5), tracked by adoption, margin and repeat orders. |

The sections below explain each sentence.

## Why the US

| Reason | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Largest data-centre market** | ≈ 50 GW operating, +19 % since 2024, pipeline ≈ 3× operating; ≈ 90 % of the Americas' build-out ([`Lucca_GoalB_proposal.md`](background/Lucca_GoalB_proposal.md) B1, [`Ali_GoalB_research.md`](background/Ali_GoalB_research.md) Q3) |
| **Schneider's largest region** | North America 39 % of revenue, +15 % organic; US and India the 2026 growth drivers; data-centre demand up by triple digits in Q4 2025, mostly US hyperscale and colocation (FY2025 results, in [`Lucca_GoalB_proposal.md`](background/Lucca_GoalB_proposal.md)) |
| **Top of the market screening** | US scores 18–19, the highest with India (18) ([`Lucca_GoalB_proposal.md`](background/Lucca_GoalB_proposal.md) B1) |
| **Customers already count SF6** | Hyperscalers have 2030 targets (Microsoft carbon negative, Meta net zero across its value chain) ([`Jessie_GoalB_research.md`](background/Jessie_GoalB_research.md) §10); SF6 is one of the seven gases in the GHG Protocol, so leaks from switchgear a company owns sit in its Scope 1 with or without a local rule ([GHG Protocol](https://ghgprotocol.org/corporate-standard)) |
| **Delay is expensive** | One month of delay costs ≈ $150–240K per MW; MV switchgear lead times are 52–104 weeks vs ≈ 24 before 2020 ([`Ali_GoalB_research.md`](background/Ali_GoalB_research.md) Q15) |

**Where to start: Texas.**

| State | Capacity | SF6 rule | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Texas** | ≈ 26 GW operating + under construction vs Virginia ≈ 13 GW (JLL midyear 2026, via Baxtel); ≈ 6.5 GW under construction, four campuses of ≈ 900 MW or more ([Baxtel](https://baxtel.com/news/2026-is-the-year-texas-passes-virginia-in-data-center-capacity)); 474 GW of large loads in ERCOT's queue, ≈ 90 % data centres ([ERCOT](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/09/07/10-Interconnection-and-Grid-Analysis-Update.pdf)) | None ([EPA state list](https://epa.gov/eps-partnership/state-and-regional-regulations-related-sf6-emissions-electric-transmission-and)) | **Lead state**: biggest new build, voluntary by definition; Schneider is adding switchgear capacity in El Paso ([MMH](https://www.mmh.com/article/schneider-electric-commits-700-million-boost-domestic-manufacturing/Automation)) |
| **Northern Virginia** | ≈ 10.1 GW operating, the largest single market (≈ 17 % of US operating capacity); fewer large new campuses ([Baxtel](https://baxtel.com/news/2026-is-the-year-texas-passes-virginia-in-data-center-capacity)) | None | Second wave: the installed base of the same customers |
| **California, New York, Massachusetts** | Smaller build-out | SF6 phase-outs in force or adopted ([`Ali_GoalB_research.md`](background/Ali_GoalB_research.md) Q5) | **Bridge only**: a customer that must go SF6-free there (or in the EU) can reuse the same spec in Texas (our inference) |

**Segment:** hyperscale and colocation data centres, on new campuses, where the switchgear choice is
being made now. Large campuses usually take power at 138–230 kV into their own substation and
distribute it at 15 kV or 34.5 kV ([TrueLook](https://www.truelook.com/blog/data-center-substation-design),
contractor blog), so the operator often owns the MV switchgear, and its SF6, itself; owning the
substation is becoming a hyperscaler strategy ([Black & Veatch](https://www.bv.com/project/substation-ownership-becomes-a-power-strategy-for-hyper-scale-data-centers)).

## B2 · Competitive landscape

| Competitor | Position in the US | SF6-free MV certified to US standards? |
|---|---|---|
| **Eaton** | US-based; Electrical Americas data-centre orders ≈ +200 % in Q4 2025 ([`Jessie_GoalB_research.md`](background/Jessie_GoalB_research.md) §10); segment margin ≈ 30 % vs Schneider Energy Management 21.8 % ([`Lucca_GoalB_proposal.md`](background/Lucca_GoalB_proposal.md) B2); new US plant for AIS and GIS from H1 2027 ([Renewable Energy World](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/?p=80798137395)) | **Not found.** Xiria (SF6-free) appears in European projects; the North American gas-insulated range (Power Xpert XGIS, up to 38 kV, UL) uses SF6 ([Eaton XGIS](https://www.eaton.com/in/en-us/catalog/medium-voltage-power-distribution-control-systems/power-xpert-xgis-gas-insulated-medium-voltage-switchgear.html)); SF6-free data-centre offer runs through a Siemens Energy partnership, marketed outside the US ([Eaton](https://www.eaton.com/gb/en-gb/markets/data-centers/eaton-and-siemens-energy.html)) |
| **Siemens, ABB** | Established MV suppliers | SF6-free ranges sold under IEC (8DJH blue, SafeRing Air); US versions not found |
| **Schneider** | Largest region; $700M in US plants to 2027, incl. a new MV facility in Mt. Juliet (TN) and more switchgear capacity in El Paso (TX) ([MMH](https://www.mmh.com/article/schneider-electric-commits-700-million-boost-domestic-manufacturing/Automation)); data-centre reference designs ([se.com](https://www.se.com/us/en/work/solutions/data-centers-and-networks/reference-designs)) | **Not confirmed.** SM / RM AirSeT are IEC ranges up to 24 kV ([se.com](https://www.se.com/nl/en/work/products/medium-voltage-switchgear-and-energy-automation/)); RM AirSeT 38 Software Defined targets 36/38 kV data-centre campuses, orders 2027, deliveries 2028, pilot at an undisclosed Equinix site ([DCD](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/schneider-electric-and-equinix-trial-software-defined-switchgear-for-data-centers)) |

- **Price:** Eaton is 5.5 % below Schneider in the benchmark, and its premium over SF6 brands fell
  from +46 % to +1 % ([`eda_findings.md`](../eda_findings.md) §11): SF6-free no longer earns a premium
  by itself. The benchmark is European and synthetic; US list prices are ≈ 2× the EU level
  ([`Lucca_GoalB_proposal.md`](background/Lucca_GoalB_proposal.md) B2).
- **Spec:** the European Xiria is rated 630 A / 21 kA and is 500 mm wide, against RM6's 400 A /
  12.5 kA and 1,186 mm ([`Lucca_GoalB_proposal.md`](background/Lucca_GoalB_proposal.md) B2, teardown specs):
  a strong product once Eaton brings it to the US.

**Where Schneider wins:**
- **first with a US-certified SF6-free range**, if it moves before Eaton's US plant and the Eaton–Siemens
  Energy offer arrive;
- **its own SM6 / RM6 data-centre accounts**: upgrading within the same supplier, service and
  engineering habits is easier than changing brand;
- **34.5 kV campuses from 2028** with RM AirSeT 38, above what IEC-rated rivals offer up to 24 kV;
- **the whole power chain**: AirSeT chosen inside Schneider's data-centre reference designs, not in a
  one-off tender;
- **made in the US**: shorter delivery and no import tariff (B4).

**Where Schneider loses:**
- standard units bought on price in one-off tenders, against Eaton's US-made range;
- any site needing US certification before Schneider has it;
- if Eaton brings an SF6-free US range first: its data-centre momentum and margin room let it
  undercut.

## B3 · Value proposition for US data centres

**Pitch: a no-regret choice. Same cost, no gas to manage, one design for every site.**

- **Lifecycle cost:** our 5-year check has AirSeT breaking even on cash at up to ≈ 5–7.5 % above
  SM6 (no SF6 monitoring, top-ups or certified handling) ([`Lucca_GoalB_proposal.md`](background/Lucca_GoalB_proposal.md)
  B3). At parity (B4), the operator keeps that saving. Every cost that differs favours AirSeT
  ([`Ali_GoalB_research.md`](background/Ali_GoalB_research.md) Q2).
- **The SF6 a unit carries:** an RM6 holds ≈ 1.5 kg of SF6 (teardown estimate; some Schneider RMUs
  0.6–0.7 kg on the rating plate, [`Jessie_GoalB_research.md`](background/Jessie_GoalB_research.md) §10).

| SF6 value per unit | CO₂e | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expected emissions over 30 years (0.1 %/yr leaks + 10 % lost at end of life) | ≈ 4.7 t | ≈ €430 at €90/t | The realistic cost ([`Ali_GoalB_research.md`](background/Ali_GoalB_research.md) Q2) |
| Everything stored in the unit | ≈ 35 t | ≈ €1,760 at €50/t | The worst case if the gas escapes ([`Lucca_GoalB_proposal.md`](background/Lucca_GoalB_proposal.md) B3) |

  Texas has no carbon price, so in the lead state this is a reporting and future-risk argument,
  not a cash one.
- **Nothing to report in Scope 1:** SF6 leaks count in the owner's Scope 1 under the GHG Protocol,
  wherever the site is. AWS, Google, Meta, Microsoft and Digital Realty jointly ask suppliers for
  Scope 3 data and product declarations ([DCD](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/aws-digital-realty-google-meta-microsoft-and-schneider-electric-call-for-greater-supplier-transparency-on-scope-3-emissions));
  an SF6-free PEP answers it.
- **No gas to manage, now or at end of life:** no SF6 inventory, leak checks or certified handling,
  and no recovery contractor at decommissioning, which carries ≈ 75 % of an RM6's lifetime SF6
  emissions ([`Ali_GoalB_research.md`](background/Ali_GoalB_research.md) Q2). A unit installed in 2027 retires
  around 2060, under whatever rules apply then.
- **One design for every site:** hyperscalers reuse a standard design and adapt it locally
  ([Vertiv](https://www.vertiv.com/498240/globalassets/documents/white-papers/vertiv-colocationmarket2023-br-en-us_380504_0.pdf));
  their EU sites must be SF6-free since 2026 and their California and New York sites are phasing it
  out, so one SF6-free spec stays valid everywhere (our inference: no source names a company with a
  global SF6-free spec).
- **Speed (from 2028):** RM AirSeT 38 Software Defined is up to 3× faster to order and build and 2×
  faster to commission than custom-built gear (Schneider estimate, [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/it-infrastructure/news/366651545/Schneider-software-defined-switchgear-targets-AI-data-center-delays)),
  worth $150–240K per MW for every month saved.

## B4 · Pricing and commercial approach

| Lever | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Parity with SM6, then a service premium** | Same price as SM6 for the first references (years 1–2); from year 3, a premium only through an optional monitoring and service package (EcoCare, vendor claim up to 40 % less maintenance), e.g. +5 % | Parity costs Schneider ≈ €75 of raw material per unit (≈ 0.6 % of the price); Eaton is already cheaper, so a visible premium hands it the references |
| **2. Quote rules + indexation** | Fixed price adjusted for the forecast steel rise, risk buffer up to 2 %, 6-month validity; an index clause on the steel and copper indices Schneider buys on lets AirSeT offer SM6's 9 months without taking the risk. Worst case (P90, +9 months): margin from ≈ 20 % to ≈ 17 % ([`pricing.md`](../pricing.md)) | Common basis for the three markets; data-centre ETO contracts lock prices for 3–9 months |
| **3. Certify and build in the US** | A US-certified (ANSI / IEEE, UL) AirSeT assembled in the US MV plants (Mt. Juliet, El Paso) | Without certification AirSeT cannot be specified; imports from Hungary face Section 232 duties (25–50 %, possibly 15 % for power equipment, classification to check, [EY](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2026-0788)) |
| **4. Default in reference designs** | AirSeT as the standard MV line in Schneider's data-centre reference designs and in the specs written by the engineering firms that design the campuses | Buyers opt out of AirSeT rather than in (our inference: no source on who writes the specs) |
| **5. Early-adopter pilots + warranty** | 2–3 pilots with operators that also run regulated sites (CA, NY, EU), with an extended warranty and published results; build on the Equinix pilot | An outage costs a data centre far more than the switchgear: references lower the trust barrier |
| **6. Take-back of old SF6 units** | Recover and destroy the SF6 of replaced units with each new order | Removes the end-of-life burden on the customer's existing fleet |

## B5 · Roadmap, KPIs and risks

**Phased entry: certify first, win references in Texas, then follow the same customers.**

| Phase | Accounts | Actions | Milestone to move on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Year 1 (2027): certify and pilot** | 2–3 hyperscale or colocation campuses in Texas, run by operators that also have sites in CA, NY or the EU | US certification of AirSeT up to 15 kV class; assembly in a US plant; pilots at parity with warranty; RM AirSeT 38 orders open | US-certified product shipping; at least one pilot energised |
| **Years 2–3 (2028–29): same customers, more sites** | The pilot customers' new campuses; Schneider SM6 / RM6 data-centre accounts | AirSeT as the default in reference designs; first RM AirSeT 38 deliveries; service package launched | Repeat orders from pilot customers; AirSeT in the reference designs |
| **Years 4–5 (2030–31): scale** | 34.5 kV campuses; Northern Virginia | RM AirSeT 38 on large campuses; enter Virginia's installed base | — |

**KPIs** (same as the other markets; targets to calibrate with Schneider). A KPI below its threshold
at the year-2/3 checkpoint is the failure condition.

| KPI | Year-5 target | Fails if (year-2/3 checkpoint) | If it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| AirSeT share of Schneider's new MV unit sales in US data centres | 25 % | below half the path to target | Shift effort to India and Southeast Asia; keep the US to existing accounts |
| AirSeT margin minus SM6 margin | ≥ −1 pt | worse than −3 pt | Revisit pricing (bring the service premium forward) before changing the plan |
| Pilot customers that buy again | ≥ 1 by year 2; most by year 5 | none by year 2 | Rework the offer with the pilot customers before scaling |

**Risks and what we do:**

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| No US-certified AirSeT, or certification takes longer than year 1 | Make it the year-1 gate; until then, sell only existing accounts and prepare RM AirSeT 38 |
| Eaton or Eaton–Siemens Energy brings an SF6-free US range first | Move fast with the Equinix pilot and existing accounts; parity price |
| Tariffs on imported units | US assembly (lever 3) |
| Texas pipeline stalls: ERCOT's queue process is under strain and the governor ordered projects verified before they advance ([Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/06/17/texas-ercot-data-center-energy-grid/), [Data Center Frontier](https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/energy/article/55401760/ercot-puts-texas-ai-megawatts-to-the-test)) | Target campuses already under construction; Virginia as the second market |
| Federal climate rules weaken | The argument rests on customers' own targets and on state and EU rules, not on federal law |
| The real AirSeT premium is far above break-even | Confirm with Schneider; the service package carries any premium |
| Cannibalising Schneider's own SM6 sales | Accepted: parity keeps the margin within ≈ 0.6 pt |
