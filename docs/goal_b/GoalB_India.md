# Goal B · India

One of three Goal B markets (with the US and Malaysia–Singapore), covering B2–B5. India is the **proof market**: the one place where customers have already
switched to SF6-free MV units with no law forcing them.

## Executive summary

| Part | Agreed sentence |
|---|---|
| **Why India** | India is the only market where utilities have already switched to SF6-free with no law forcing them (Tata Power, BSES Rajdhani), it is Schneider's 2026 growth driver alongside the US and its largest emerging hub, and it is modernising its grid (~€28 bn) while its data-centre market, still small, doubles. |
| **B2 · Competition** | Schneider is the SF6-free pioneer in India, with the only utility deployments (Tata Power, BSES Rajdhani), in a niche still small and driven by private utilities. Most of the market, the state-owned utilities (private utilities serve only ~7 % of consumption), still buys SF6 at the lowest price, but it is modernising with the ~€28 bn distribution scheme. Competition is real: Lucy Electric, the RMU leader, already has an SF6-free unit, and Siemens and ABB have one globally. Schneider's lead is a head start, not a given: with the market growing and modernising, this window must not be lost. |
| **B3 · Value proposition** | At the same price as SM6, AirSeT costs an Indian utility less to own (no SF6 to monitor, top up or recover), is already proven by Tata Power and BSES Rajdhani, and removes SF6 from the Scope 1 that listed utilities must now report with assurance. |
| **B4 · Pricing and commercial** | In India, AirSeT is priced at parity with SM6 under the quote rules, made in Schneider's Indian plants to compete in public tenders with no currency risk, and enters through private utilities with the smart offer and the Tata Power and BSES Rajdhani cases, while Schneider works to make SF6-free the default in utility specifications. |
| **B5 · Roadmap and win conditions** | Over 2027–2031, Schneider turns its Indian SF6-free pilots into references with private utilities (year 1), scales with more private utilities and data centres while it localises AirSeT and gets SF6-free into utility specs (years 2–3), then enters state-owned utility tenders with a locally made AirSeT (years 4–5), tracked by adoption, margin and repeat orders. |

The sections below explain each sentence.

## Why India

| Reason | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Voluntary switching has already happened here** | Tata Power ordered 11 kV SF6-free RM AirSeT units for Mumbai and Delhi (2025), with Odisha next; Schneider claims up to 50 % lifecycle saving and 75 % lower carbon footprint. BSES Rajdhani runs India's first SF6-free *smart* RMU pilot (RM AirSeT 12 kV + T300 remote terminal). No law required either |
| **Schneider's strongest emerging hub** | 100 % of its Indian subsidiary since Dec 2025 (€5.5 bn for the last 35 %); bought L&T's Electrical & Automation business in 2020 (₹14,000 crore, now the Lauritz Knudsen brand); listed MV arm with 4 plants (Vadodara ×2, Kolkata, Chennai). India and the US are the group's 2026 growth drivers |
| **Grid money is flowing** | Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme: ₹2.83 lakh crore (~€28 bn) sanctioned for distribution upgrades; ~36–41 % done, extended to FY2027-28 |
| **Data-centre growth (small base)** | Still small: ~1.6–1.8 GW operating (mid-2026), ~3–4 % of the US. But fast: 387 MW added in 2025 (+103 %), > 600 MW expected in 2026; 4–6.5 GW by 2030. Mumbai ~50 % and Chennai ~20 % of capacity; hyperscalers committing > $50 bn |
| **Healthy macro** | GDP +7.0 % (IMF 2026), policy rate 5.50 %, rupee stable vs € (+1 % in a year) |
| **Disclosure pressure without a ban** | SEBI's BRSR: the top 1,000 listed companies report Scope 1 and 2, with reasonable assurance for all 1,000 from FY2026-27. SF6 leaks are Scope 1 under the GHG Protocol, so listed utilities (Tata Power, Adani, Torrent, CESC) and data-centre owners must count them |
| **The window is closing** | Lucy Electric India dispatched its first SF6-free RMU (AegisEcoTec, SWICON'25). Schneider is first, but no longer alone |

**Segments:** utilities are the **core** (grid money and the voluntary switches are there); data
centres are the **growth** segment, an upside rather than the reason to choose India.

**Where to start inside India:** Delhi and Mumbai (private utilities with SF6-free references) and
the data-centre hubs of Mumbai and Chennai.

## B2 · Competitive landscape

In India the main rival is **not Eaton** (the US threat) but **Lucy Electric**, the incumbent RMU
supplier to Indian utilities.

| Competitor | Position in India | SF6-free offer in India |
|---|---|---|
| **Lucy Electric India** | 25+ years in India; says it is **#1 in RMUs with 100,000+ units installed** (company claim, 2022); manufactures in Gujarat | AegisEcoTec SF6-free RMU, **first units dispatched 2025** |
| **Schneider** | Largest electrical footprint in India (L&T E&A, Lauritz Knudsen); listed MV arm with 4 plants | RM AirSeT: Tata Power order (Mumbai, Delhi), BSES Rajdhani smart pilot. **First with utility references**; units still imported |
| **Siemens, ABB, Hitachi Energy (India)** | Established MV suppliers | Siemens 8DJH blue and ABB SafeRing Air exist globally; no India launch found |
| **Local makers** (e.g. RMC Switchgears, Coronet) | Win state tenders as lowest bidder | SF6 only; 11 kV RMUs listed at ₹2.5–3.5 lakh (~€2.5–3.4k) |

**What the tenders show:** state utility tenders still specify SF6, e.g. BEST Mumbai's 2026 tender
for "11 kV 250 MVA outdoor **SF6 insulated** RMU", and are awarded to the lowest bidder. An
SF6-free unit cannot even bid unless the spec allows it.

**Margins are thin:** Hitachi Energy India ~10 % EBITDA; Schneider's listed MV arm ~14.5 % EBIT.

**Where Schneider wins:**
- private utilities that buy on lifecycle and reporting, not lowest price (Tata Power, BSES);
- the smart offer (AirSeT + remote terminal), which fits the grid-automation push;
- references: the only SF6-free *utility* deployments in India so far.

**Where Schneider loses:**
- lowest-price state tenders written for SF6;
- tenders that require Indian-made equipment, while AirSeT is imported;
- head-to-head with Lucy, which has the installed base and now an SF6-free unit.

## B3 · Value proposition for Indian utilities and data centres

**Pitch: lifecycle cost, proven at home, and nothing to explain in the Scope 1 report.**

- **Lifecycle cost: up to 50 % saving over the units' lifespan for Tata Power**
  ([Schneider × Tata Power](https://www.se.com/in/en/about-us/newsroom/news/press-releases/tata-power-schneider-electric-join-hands-in-india%E2%80%99s-journey-towards-sustainable-and-efficient-power-distribution-with-sf6-free-ring-main-units-67b30b336be827011b0835f2)). Our own 5-year check, at the Indian price
  (below): AirSeT breaks even on cash at up to ~5–7.5 % above SM6 (no SF6 monitoring, top-ups,
  certified handling). At parity (B4), the utility saves ~€320–480 per unit within 5 years. The SF6 a
  unit carries (35 t CO₂e, ~€1,760 at €50/t) is ~28 % of the Indian price.
- **Indian price base:** an 11 kV 3-way smart SF6 RMU (motorised, 1 circuit breaker + 2 switches,
  remote terminal) is listed at **₹6.45 lakh (~€6.3k)** in a state utility's unit-rate schedule
  ([DHBVN, Haryana](https://dhbvn.org.in/staticContent/new/smartgridprojects/Gurgaon-1to57/Ann_C_Unit_rates_DLF_estimate.pdf); a project estimate from a few years ago, so today's price is likely
  €6–8k). That is about half the European price (€12.2k), for a higher-spec unit. Dealer listings at
  ₹2.5–3.5 lakh are simpler units without automation.
- **Modernisation moment:** India is investing ~€28 bn (₹2.83 lakh crore) to modernise its
  distribution networks through a central government scheme, extended to FY2027-28. Utilities are
  replacing equipment now, the moment when switching to SF6-free costs least.
- **No carbon price yet:** India's carbon market does not cover utilities' SF6, so the SF6 liability
  is a reporting and future-risk argument in India, not a cash one.
- **Proven in India:** Tata Power and BSES Rajdhani are references an Indian utility engineer
  trusts more than a European case study.
- **Disclosure:** listed utilities and data-centre owners report Scope 1 with assurance under
  BRSR; an SF6-free fleet removes a line they would otherwise have to measure and defend.
- **Data centres (growth segment):** global cloud companies with sites in Mumbai and Chennai must
  already buy SF6-free in Europe; AirSeT lets them use one design in both places (our inference: no
  source names a company with a global SF6-free spec). India's data-centre market is still small
  (~3–4 % of the US), so this is a second front behind utilities.

## B4 · Pricing and commercial approach

| Lever | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Parity with SM6** | Same price as SM6 | India is price-sensitive. Costs Schneider ~€75 of raw material per unit (~1.2 % of the ~€6.3k Indian price); the utility keeps the maintenance savings (B3) |
| **2. Quote rules** | Fixed price adjusted for the forecast steel rise, risk buffer up to 2 %, 6-month validity; indexation on the steel index Schneider buys on only beyond that. Worst case (P90, +9 months), at the Indian price: ~€330 per unit, margin from ~20 % to ~15 % (from Schneider India's ~14.5 % operating margin, to ~9 %). Local assembly and indexation protect it | Common basis for the three markets |
| **3. Make it in India** | Assemble AirSeT in Schneider's existing Indian MV plants | Qualifies as a local supplier in public tenders (Make in India preference); cuts cost and lead time; removes currency risk (rupee cost, rupee price) |
| **4. Make SF6-free the default spec** | Identify where utility specs and tenders require SF6 (e.g. BEST Mumbai 2026: "SF6 insulated RMU"), and push SF6-free as the default with utility engineering teams and the technical regulator, using the Tata Power and BSES results | Today SF6 is written into state tenders, so AirSeT cannot even bid; once SF6-free is the default, buyers opt out of it rather than in |
| **5. Reference programme** | Measure and publish the Tata Power and BSES Rajdhani savings; offer pilots on the same terms to other utilities | B3's "proven in India" argument |
| **6. Smart offer** | AirSeT with remote terminal and monitoring, as in the BSES pilot | Year-1 entry offer; fits the automation utilities are buying |

## B5 · Roadmap, KPIs and risks

**Phased entry: start with private accounts, then move to state utilities once AirSeT is made
locally and accepted in their specs.**

| Phase | Accounts | Actions | Milestone to move on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Year 1 (2027): references** | Tata Power (Delhi, Mumbai), BSES Rajdhani / Yamuna, Adani Electricity Mumbai; data-centre colocation in Mumbai and Chennai | Enter with the smart offer (AirSeT + remote terminal); measure and publish pilot savings; map which utility specs require SF6; start local assembly | Published savings from at least one pilot |
| **Years 2–3: private scale-up** | Tata Power's Odisha utilities, Torrent Power, CESC; first data-centre references | Parity pricing and monitoring bundle; push SF6-free as the default spec with utility engineering teams and the technical regulator | AirSeT assembled in India; at least one state utility spec accepts SF6-free |
| **Years 4–5: state utilities** | State-owned utilities through public tenders | Bid the locally made smart offer in automation and loss-reduction tenders under Make in India | — |

**KPIs** (same as the other markets; targets to calibrate with Schneider). A KPI below its threshold
at the year-2/3 checkpoint is the failure condition.

| KPI | Year-5 target | Fails if (year-2/3 checkpoint) | If it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| AirSeT share of Schneider's new MV unit sales in India | 30 % | below half the path to target | Shift resources to the fallback market (China) or bring Brazil forward |
| AirSeT margin minus SM6 margin | ≥ −1.5 pt (parity costs ~1.2 pt at the Indian price) | worse than −5.5 pt (beyond the quote rules' worst case at the Indian price, −5.2 pt) | Revisit pricing (leave parity) before changing the plan |
| Pilot utilities that buy again | ≥ 1 by year 2; most by year 5 | none by year 2 | Rework the offer with the pilot utilities before scaling |

**Risks and what we do:**

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| Price: local SF6 RMUs far cheaper | Parity with SM6, lifecycle-cost quote, local assembly |
| Lucy Electric wins the next references | Move fast with the private utilities already engaged |
| State specs stay SF6-only | Keep pushing the default-spec lever; stay on private utilities and data centres meanwhile |
| Public tenders require Indian-made equipment | Local AirSeT assembly by year 3 |
| Cannibalising Schneider's own SM6 / RM6 sales | Accepted: parity keeps the margin within ~1.2 pt |
