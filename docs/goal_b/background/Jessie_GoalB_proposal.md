# Jessie Goal B proposal

My independent proposal for B1–B5 (methodology §5), to compare with the team on Tue 24/11.
Facts, data and sources: [`Jessie_GoalB_research.md`](Jessie_GoalB_research.md) (§ below refers to it).

**In one sentence:** win the voluntary switch by **lowering the customer's perceived risk**:
start where Schneider's win rate is highest and build reference cases, sell AirSeT as
**future-proofing** for equipment that runs 30–40 years, and price it **close to competitors**,
backed by Schneider's support and an indexation clause that removes raw-material risk.

## B1 · Target markets & segments
**Logic:** successful references lower the perceived risk for the next customers, so start where
the win rate should be highest, then move to the largest growth markets.

| Order | Markets | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **US data centers** (California first) and the **UK** | California already phases out SF6 in new gas-insulated equipment (CARB): a regulated foothold and proof that rules spread. The US is the largest data center market and Schneider's major market (Q&A 18/11). The UK is reviewing SF6 and published a pathway to SF6-free distribution networks; Q&A names it a likely next step |
| 1 | **Companies linked to the EU** (data centers serving EU workloads, exporters supplying EU customers) | The EU banned SF6 in new MV switchgear ≤ 24 kV from Jan 2026. Under CSRD, in-scope companies report value-chain (Scope 3) emissions and send data requests to non-EU suppliers, so EU-facing customers feel the pressure without a local ban |
| 2 | **Fastest data center growth:** India, Southeast Asia (Malaysia / Johor), Middle East | India 2.2 → 12 GW by 2030; Southeast Asia plans ≈ 4× its running capacity (Malaysia > 6 GW planned); Middle East 1.3 → 6.3 GW by 2030 |
| Watch | **Morocco** | Large projects (Dakhla 500 MW, Tetouan 386–500 MW) largely aimed at European workloads, which fits the EU-link logic; but projects are early or uncertain (feasibility studies, a developer with no track record) and Dakhla is in Western Sahara (political risk) |
| Watch | **China** | Major Schneider market (Q&A); State Grid is piloting SF6-free high-voltage equipment (carbon neutrality 2060), but no medium-voltage rule yet |

**Segments:** data center operators (hyperscalers and colocation) first; new builds and
end-of-life replacements, where a switchgear choice is being made now.

Europe itself is out of scope (the switch is mandatory there), but Schneider's EU volume gives the
references and production scale that this strategy relies on.

## B2 · Competition
SF6-free medium voltage is no longer unique to Schneider (§10):

| Competitor | SF6-free MV offer | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Eaton | Xiria (air-insulated, since 2002), > 150,000 panels sold | Revenue $27.4B (2025); data center orders in the Americas ≈ +200 % in Q4 2025 |
| ABB | SafeRing Air / SafePlus Air (since 2020) | Same footprint and interface as its SF6 models: easy swap |
| Siemens, Hitachi Energy | Siemens blue GIS; Hitachi mainly high voltage | To check for Siemens MV |

- In our price data, Eaton's premium over SF6 brands fell from +46 % (2017) to +1 % (2026) and it
  is now 5.5 % cheaper than Schneider (§5): SF6-free no longer earns a premium by itself.
- So Schneider's premium can only rest on brand, reliability and support, and should stay small
  while expanding; it can be higher with loyal existing customers.

## B3 · Value proposition
**Main message: future risk, not yearly savings.**

- **Long life:** switchgear runs 30–40 years (AirSeT up to 40, Schneider's claim). Equipment bought
  today still runs in 2050, when rules are likely stricter: the EU has banned SF6, California is
  phasing it out, the UK is reviewing, China is piloting.
- **A stored liability:** an MV unit holds up to a few kg of SF6 (typically < 5 kg); 1 kg ≈ 23.5–24.3
  t CO₂e. A unit with 2 kg stores ≈ 48 t CO₂e. Sealed units leak < 0.1 % a year (≈ 50 kg CO₂e),
  so yearly savings are small; the risk is the stored amount: accidents, end-of-life recovery,
  future reporting and handling rules. AirSeT has no such liability.
- **The owner's emissions:** SF6 leaking from switchgear the customer owns counts in the customer's
  own direct emissions (Scope 1, fugitive gases; GHG Protocol wording to confirm), and data center operators have public climate targets
  (Microsoft carbon negative by 2030, Meta net zero across its value chain by 2030). These targets
  are voluntary, not binding.
- **Carbon pricing is starting to cover SF6:** Singapore's carbon tax includes SF6 (S$45 / t in
  2026, S$50–80 targeted by 2030), though only for facilities above 25,000 t CO₂e a year. A future
  risk, not a cost most customers pay today.
- **Small cost difference on materials:** AirSeT's raw material costs ≈ €75 more per unit than
  RM6, ≈ 0.6 % of the price (§3). The full price gap is not in our data.
- **Best timing:** customers building new sites or replacing end-of-life equipment.

## B4 · Commercial, pricing and adoption levers
- **Price close to competitors:** at most a reasonable premium justified by brand and loyalty;
  near parity for new customers while expanding (B2).
- **Sell the support, which lowers perceived risk:** Schneider's engineering for customised (ETO)
  orders, plus AirSeT's digital monitoring (thermal, environmental, remote app, circuit-breaker
  wear alerts) and EcoCare services (Schneider claims up to 40 % less maintenance); vacuum
  interrupters are sealed for life.
- **Phased migration:** SM is modular, so a customer can replace or add AirSeT cubicles step by
  step instead of the whole switchboard.
- **Use our raw-material forecast:** AirSeT's raw-material risk is slightly higher, so P4's rule
  keeps an AirSeT quote valid 6 months vs 9 for RM6 (§4). An **indexation clause** on public
  indices (LME copper, steel coil) lets Schneider offer AirSeT the same validity as SM6 without
  taking the raw-material risk, which removes one reason to prefer the SF6 product.

## B5 · Roadmap & win conditions
| Phase | What | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · References | Pilot projects with a few data center accounts in the US (California first), the UK and EU-linked customers | Reference sites that lower perceived risk |
| 2 · Growth markets | Use the references in India, Southeast Asia and the Middle East; target new builds and replacements | Volume |
| 3 · Scale | Make AirSeT the default offer; review Morocco and China as regulation and projects mature | Share |

**KPIs**
- Win rate (offers accepted ÷ proposals), for existing and new customers separately.
- SF6-free share of sales, in units and in revenue, and its trend.
- Number of reference sites; countries entered.
- Margin of AirSeT deals (link to Goal A: growth must not cost margin).

**What would make it fail**
- Competitors (Eaton, ABB) undercut on price and win the references first.
- Regulation outside the EU arrives later than expected, so the future-risk argument weakens.
- Customers do not value ESG enough to change their switchgear choice.
- The AirSeT cost gap widens (raw materials or production), so near-parity pricing erodes margin.
