# Goal B proposal: winning a voluntary SM6 → SM AirSeT switch (Lucca)

Independent proposal for B1–B5 ([`methodology.md`](../../methodology.md) §5), written before reading the
other proposals, for the team comparison on Tue 24/11.

## Summary

No law forces the switch in the markets we target, so each market needs its own reason to switch.
Start where Schneider has already proven a voluntary switch (India), and keep AirSeT priced close to
SM6: over 5 years it only beats SM6 on cash if the premium stays under ~5–7.5 %.

| Part | In one sentence |
|---|---|
| **B1 Markets** | Wave 1: Indian utilities and US data centres (in states with no SF6 ban yet); wave 2: Brazilian data centres; fallback: Chinese private data centres. |
| **B2 Competition** | India first: Schneider was first there with SF6-free units and we found no SF6-free rival. The US is the biggest prize but Eaton Xiria is already sold there. Brazil has ABB and Siemens SF6-free plus WEG. In China, centralised buying favours state-owned makers. |
| **B3 Value** | 5-year cost of ownership: AirSeT breaks even on cash at up to ~5–7.5 % above SM6 (no SF6 handling); counting the SF6 the customer carries (35 t CO₂e per unit), up to ~20 %. The pitch changes by country. |
| **B4 Pricing** | P4's quote rules (fixed price, steel-forecast adjustment, buffer ≤ 2 %, 6-month validity). Parity with SM6 in India, Brazil and China; +5 % in US data centres with a service package. One adoption lever per country. |
| **B5 Roadmap** | 2027–2031: pilots → references → scale-up. Three KPIs (adoption, AirSeT vs SM6 margin, pilots that buy again); missing one at the year-2/3 checkpoint triggers the fallback. |

## The question

In markets where no law forces SF6 out, how do we get customers to switch from SM6 (SF6) to
SM AirSeT (SF6-free) voluntarily?

### Why customers need convincing

- **The cost gap is Schneider's, not the customer's.** On priced raw material (v1 BOM), AirSeT costs
  **~€75 more** than RM6 (€975 vs €900), ~0.6 % of a ~€12k price. The gap shrank from +€264 (Apr 2022)
  to +€73 (Aug 2026) as copper rose. It leaves out manufacturing, vacuum interrupters, engineering and
  the AirSeT lines with no price series ("Other"), which would add to AirSeT's cost.
  - Note: `methodology.md` §5 still says RM6 is "€98 dearer". That came from an older BOM.
- **Without regulation, the cost of SF6 is invisible.** The customer pays nothing for leaks,
  reporting or end-of-life recovery.
- **Switching costs more than the price:**
  - teams know SM6 and how to maintain it;
  - spare parts are already in stock;
  - purchase specs and utility approvals were written around it;
  - new technology is a risk for the buyer.
- **For Schneider, parity is cheap:** selling AirSeT at the SM6 price costs ~€75 of margin per unit
  (~0.6 % of the price), small against a ~20 % margin.

All cost and price data are for **RM6 and RM AirSeT** (compact RMUs), not SM6 and SM AirSeT. We use
them as a proxy and say so.

## B1 Target markets and segments

Approach: decide **where to play** first, then **how to win**. Countries are chosen on data only; the
Q&A hints (US, China, UK) were not used as a reason to include or exclude a country.

## B3 Value proposition

**What convinces each customer:**

| Country (segment) | Pitch |
|---|---|
| India (utilities) | Lifecycle cost, backed by existing references (Tata Power, BSES Rajdhani) |
| US (data centres) | Emission targets (Scope 1/2/3), risk that the state bans SF6 next, footprint, lead time |
| Brazil (data centres) | On a clean grid, SF6 is a bigger share of the emissions left to cut; plus price |
| China (private DCs) | CO₂ and total cost |

**5-year cost of ownership per unit, SM6 vs AirSeT** (illustrative):

| Component | Assumption | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price P | Schneider EU price €12.2k for every country, range 0.7×–1.3×, until Q&A #23 | EDA §11; World Bank ICP 2021 |
| AirSeT premium over SM6 | scenarios 0 / +10 / +20 % (makers quote 5–25 %) | supplier sites; #23 |
| Extra SF6 maintenance and handling | 1–1.5 % of P per year | derived from Eaton's 30-year TCO model (a competitor): SF6 costs 45–57 % of P more over life |
| Carbon on leaks | 35 t CO₂e × 0.1–0.5 %/yr × local carbon price (California ~€30/t; China ~€11.5/t; India and Brazil none) | teardown; CARB; China ETS |
| SF6 liability at year 5 (second line) | 35 t CO₂e × €50/t ≈ €1,760 | assumption |

**Maximum AirSeT premium that still breaks even over 5 years** (P = €12.2k):

| Line | Break-even premium |
|---|---|
| 1. Cash only | **5–7.5 %** |
| 2. Counting the SF6 liability at €50/t | **19–22 %** (16–19 % if P = 1.3×; 26–28 % if P = 0.7×) |
| 2b. Liability at the local carbon price | China (€406) 8–11 %; California (€1,050) 14–16 %; India and Brazil: no carbon price |

- Over 5 years, carbon from leaks is negligible (< €30 per unit; leak rate < 0.1 %/yr per Siemens's
  datasheet). The SF6 risk sits at end of life, in accidents and in the customer's Scope 1 inventory.
- On cash alone, AirSeT only wins at a premium of ~5–7.5 % at most, which sets up B4.
- Tata Power's "up to 50 % lifecycle saving" is Schneider's own claim, with no breakdown.

## B4 Commercial and pricing

**Quote rules: P4's fixed-price rules** ([`pricing.md`](../../pricing.md), step 5):

| Rule | AirSeT | RM6 |
|---|---|---|
| Price adjustment | builds in the forecast steel rise (~4–5 % in 6 months, P2 model) | copper on the random walk |
| Risk buffer (+3 / +6 / +9) | 0.7 / 1.3 / 2.0 % of price | 0.6 / 1.1 / 1.8 % |
| Quote validity | 6 months | 9 months |
| Indexation trigger (beyond validity or in long contracts) | steel/stainless > ±85 %; copper ±68 % | copper ±50 % |

- Steel is easier to forecast than copper (our models beat the random walk by ~10 % at 3–6 months),
  so AirSeT's fixed price sits closer to its real cost. The buffer does not shrink: all models share
  the same bands.
- The idea of a "longer validity for AirSeT" does not hold: P4 gives AirSeT 6 months vs 9 for RM6,
  because steel is forecast to rise and AirSeT carries more of it.
- Beyond validity, sell on the same steel index Schneider buys on (P4's back-to-back indexation).

**AirSeT price vs SM6, by country:**

| Country | AirSeT price | Margin effect per unit (€12.2k price) |
|---|---|---|
| India | parity | −€75 (~0.6 % of price) |
| Brazil | parity | −€75 |
| US | +5 % | +€540 (+€612 price − €75 material) |
| China (fallback) | parity (price pressure) | −€75 |

**Stress test** (P4, P90 at +9 months): at parity AirSeT loses up to €256 + €75 ≈ €330 (~2.7 % of
price), so a 20 % margin falls to ~17 %.

**Adoption levers:**

| Country | Lever |
|---|---|
| India | Reference programme with utilities: extend Tata Power and BSES Rajdhani with pilots, measured savings and published results |
| US | Early-adopter programme for data centres + digital monitoring and maintenance package (justifies the +5 %); needs a US-certified AirSeT first (Q&A #22) |
| Brazil | Financing or instalments bundled with data-centre sales (Selic 13.75 %) |
| China | None for now (fallback) |

## B5 Roadmap and win conditions

**Horizon: 5 years, 2027–2031**, consistent with B3.

| Phase | India (utilities) | US (data centres) | Brazil (data centres) | China (fallback) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1: pilots | Measure and publish Tata Power and BSES Rajdhani savings | US certification; 2–3 pilot data centres | — | — |
| Years 2–3: references | Take the case to other utilities | Published cases | Enters when its economic outlook score reaches 2 | Enters if India or the US fails |
| Years 4–5: scale-up | State utility tenders | More data centres in states with no ban | Sales with financing | Private data centres |

**Three KPIs, the same for every country. Failure = a KPI below its threshold.** Targets are
illustrative, to calibrate with Schneider.

| KPI | Measures | Year-5 target | Fails if, at the year-2/3 checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adoption** | AirSeT share of Schneider's new MV unit sales in the country | India 30 %, US 25 %, Brazil 10 % | below half of the path to target |
| **Margin** | AirSeT margin minus SM6 margin | ≥ −1 pt (parity costs ~0.6 pt) | worse than −3 pt (beyond P4's worst case, −2.7 pt) |
| **Proof** | Pilot customers who buy again | ≥ 1 per country by year 2; most pilots by year 5 | no pilot buys again by year 2 |

**On failure:**
- India or the US fails → move resources to China (fallback) or bring Brazil forward.
- Margin fails → revisit B4 pricing (leave parity) before changing the plan.

**Brazil entry trigger:** its B1 economic outlook score reaches 2 (policy rate ≤ 8 % or a stable real
against the euro).

## Caveats

- Cost and price data are for RM6 / RM AirSeT, not SM6 / SM AirSeT.
- The ~€75 cost gap leaves out unpriced lines.
- The EU competitor price benchmark is synthetic; local prices are indicative only.
- The extra SF6 maintenance cost comes from a competitor's (Eaton) model; €50/t is our assumption.
- Several country facts come from vendor or secondary sources (China regulation, SF6-free rivals,
