# Ali Goal B proposal

My proposal for B1–B5, to compare with the team on Tue 24/11. The evidence and sources behind every
point are in [`Ali_GoalB_research.md`](Ali_GoalB_research.md) (Q numbers below). Sub-parts follow
methodology §5.

**Question:** in markets with no SF6 rules, how does Schneider get customers to switch from SM6 to
SM AirSeT voluntarily?

**One-line answer:** the cost case is already won (AirSeT costs about the same over its life), so
make AirSeT the default for data centres, starting in the US, and remove the trust barrier with
references and guarantees.

---

## B1. Target markets and segments
**Theme:** who to go after first, and where.

**Proposal:** data centres first (hyperscale and colocation), in the US first, the UK next, then
SE Asia and the Gulf through the same customers' new sites. Utilities, industry and buildings come
later, not never.

**Supporting points**
- Data centres are the only segment that wants SF6-free without a law:
  - hyperscalers (AWS, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Digital Realty) jointly ask suppliers for Scope 3
    data and EPDs (Q3, Q4);
  - the market grows +25.7%/yr and 90%+ of new capacity is pre-leased, so the money and demand are
    there (slides 13–14);
  - they already lose ~$150–240K per MW for every month of delay, so they value more than price
    (Q15).
- Data centres are 30% of Schneider's 2025 orders, its largest segment (slide 7).
- Why the others come later (Q18):

| Segment | Why later |
|---|---|
| Utilities | Mostly switch only if forced (83%, vendor survey), though Indian private utilities are an exception; they order up to 5 years ahead |
| Industry (27% of orders) | Price is the main barrier; no public switchgear ESG commitments found |
| Buildings (29%) | Slower market (2–5%/yr), many small price-led buyers |
| Infrastructure (14%) | Smallest; public tenders |

- Why these countries (Q3):

| Market | Why |
|---|---|
| US first | ≈ 90% of Americas data-centre build-out; SF6 rules already in California, New York, Massachusetts, so buyers there must switch anyway |
| UK next | Phase-out planned; Schneider sees it as the likely next market |
| SE Asia, Gulf | Fast build-out; reached through the same hyperscalers' new sites rather than new accounts |
| China later | Largest SF6 emitter but unregulated; no near-term pull |

## B2. Competition
**Theme:** who else sells SF6-free, and where Schneider wins.

**Proposal:** Schneider can't win on the product itself, because rivals sell the same thing. It
wins on the situation: upgrade its own SM6 / RM6 data-centre accounts first, cover the voltages
rivals don't (38 kV), build AirSeT into its data-centre designs, and price close to Eaton so price
never decides the deal.

**Supporting points**
- **The product is not a differentiator.** Siemens (8DJH blue GIS), ABB (SafeRing Air) and Eaton
  (Xiria) sell air + vacuum units with the same footprint and the same emissions story (Q7).
- **Eaton is the main threat:** 5.5% below Schneider in the benchmark, premium over SF6 brands down
  from +46% to +1%, and on the market since 2001–02 (Q1, Q7).

| Where Schneider wins | Why | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Its own SM6 / RM6 accounts | Staying with the same supplier, service and engineering habits is easier than changing brand; the risk is that a buyer who goes SF6-free shops around | Installed base in data centres not quantified (inference) |
| Sites above 24 kV | Eaton Xiria and Siemens 8DJH blue GIS are listed up to 24 kV; RM AirSeT 38 covers the higher voltages US data centres use | Orders only from 2027 |
| Whole power chain | Schneider sells MV, LV, UPS, cooling, software and data-centre reference designs, so AirSeT is chosen at design stage, not in a one-off tender | ABB, Siemens and Eaton also sell broad ranges |
| Delivery | New capacity in the US ($700M), Hungary and Saudi Arabia while MV lead times are 52–104 weeks (Q15, Q18) | Schneider's lead times vs rivals not confirmed |

- **Where Schneider loses:** standard ≤ 24 kV units bought on price in a one-off tender. That is
  Eaton's ground; don't fight it with a premium.
- **Check with Schneider:** the size of the SM6 installed base in data centres, and current lead
  times vs rivals.

## B3. Value proposition
**Theme:** why a data centre should choose AirSeT.

**Proposal:** a no-regret choice: the same lifetime cost as SM6, one design valid everywhere for
30–40 years, and it's what their Scope 3 reporting asks for.

**Supporting points**
- **Near-parity cost (Goal A link):** raw material +€75 (~0.6% of price), down from +€264 in 2022;
  our forecast keeps the gap at €58–118 over 9 months (Q1, Q6).
- **TCO the same or lower, never higher:** every cost that differs favours AirSeT: no gas handling,
  no mandatory SF6 recovery at end of life, no 2035 EU servicing-gas risk, ~€430 of carbon avoided
  where carbon is priced (Q2, Q19).
- **No gas to manage, now or at end of life:** no SF6 inventory, leak checks, certified staff or
  handling equipment in service, and no recovery and destruction contractor at decommissioning
  (Q2). End of life is ≈ 75% of an RM6's lifetime SF6 emissions (≈ 10% of the gas lost). The unit
  is retired 30–40 years from now under whatever rules exist then, so a buyer in an unregulated
  market today may still face mandatory recovery; with AirSeT that obligation never arises. Recovery
  cost per unit is not public: ask Schneider.
- **Scope 1 and Scope 3 fit:** the GHG Protocol counts SF6 among the seven gases a company must
  report, so SF6 leaks sit in a net-zero company's Scope 1 whether or not the country bans SF6 (Q4); a
  product declaration with no SF6 answers what hyperscalers already ask suppliers for (Q4).
- **Future-proofing and one global design:** hyperscalers reuse one standard design; EU sites must
  be SF6-free since 2026, so one SF6-free spec stays valid in the EU, US states and wherever rules
  come next, for the unit's whole life (Q5, Q16).
- **Speed (roadmap):** RM AirSeT 38 Software Defined is up to 3× faster to order and build and 2×
  faster to commission (Schneider estimate; orders 2027, deliveries 2028) (Q18).

## B4. Commercial, pricing and adoption levers
**Theme:** how to price and sell it so it becomes the default.

**Proposal:** price AirSeT at or near SM6, make it the default line in data-centre quotes, sell
P4's price validity and indexation as risk protection, and lower the trust barrier with pilots,
references and warranty. Outside regulated markets, rely on customers' own climate commitments and
on specs that travel from their regulated sites.

**Supporting points**
- **How adoption spreads without a law:**
  - **Customers regulate themselves:** net-zero companies must report SF6 in Scope 1 under the GHG
    Protocol, so for them the "unregulated" market is still regulated by their own targets (Q3, Q4).
  - **Specs travel:** a customer that must go SF6-free in California, New York or the EU tends to
    reuse the same design in Texas, Malaysia or Saudi Arabia, so regulated sites are the way into
    unregulated ones (Q16; inference).
  - **Default where specs are written:** in Schneider's data-centre reference designs and with the
    engineering firms that draw the electrical plans, so the buyer opts out of AirSeT rather than in
    (inference: no source on who writes the specs).
  - **Lower the trust barrier:** pilots, references and warranty, since an outage costs a data
    centre far more than the switchgear (Q19).
- **Pricing:** the cost gap is too small to justify a visible premium; Eaton is already cheaper;
  buyers' stated tolerance (+16–20%) leaves room only if TCO is shown (Q8).
- **Quote rules from Goal A (P4):** AirSeT validity 6 months, RM6 9 months; indexation worked in the
  2021 replay while fixed buffers failed; index clauses on the indices Schneider buys on, back to
  back with suppliers (Q8). Copper and nickel come from concentrated, policy-exposed countries,
  which makes indexation worth more (Q17).
- **Levers matched to barriers (Q10, Q19):**

| Barrier | Lever |
|---|---|
| SF6 is the default spec | AirSeT as the default in reference designs and quotes |
| Quotes compare unit price only | TCO-based quote showing avoided gas handling, end-of-life recovery, carbon and regulation |
| New-technology risk | Pilot accounts, reference sites, extended warranty, monitoring bundle |
| Old SF6 fleet | Take-back of old SF6 units with new orders |
| Price risk until delivery | Index-linked pricing, so AirSeT can match SM6's validity (P4: AirSeT 6 months, RM6 9) |

## B5. Roadmap and win conditions
**Theme:** in what order, how to measure it, and when it fails.

**Proposal:** pilot with ESG-committed data-centre buyers in the US states that already regulate
SF6, turn them into references, then follow those customers to new sites and move into industry
and buildings once the references exist.

**Supporting points**

| Phase | Where | KPIs |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot | Hyperscale and colocation sites in CA, NY, MA; UK | Pilot accounts, AirSeT share of their orders, win rate |
| References | Same customers' new sites in SE Asia and the Gulf | Reference sites, repeat orders, CO₂e avoided |
| Scale | Industrial campuses, large buildings; utilities as rules arrive | AirSeT share by region, margin vs SM6, lead time |

- 2028 is the natural step-up: RM AirSeT 38 Software Defined starts delivering (Q18).
- **Fails if** (Q11): the real AirSeT premium is far above 16–20%; Eaton or others undercut;
  regulation stalls outside the EU; data-centre pipelines aren't built (82% of APAC still planned);
  AirSeT lead times are longer than SF6 gear; copper falls sharply and the cost gap widens.
